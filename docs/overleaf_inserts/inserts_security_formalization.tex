Unfolding Definition~\ref{def:zkvm-cred-adv}, the experiments defining
$\mathsf{Adv}^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}$ for
$\bullet\in\{\mathsf{unf},\mathsf{anon},\mathsf{unlink}\}$ are as follows. We
first fix the proved object and the zkVM interface, then the oracles, then the
three games; each game is the corresponding experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} evaluated on BDEC instantiated with PLUM and the zkVM,
so BDEC's Theorems~1--3 bounds apply to them unchanged.

\paragraph{The proved object: a zero-knowledge proof of knowledge of a valid signature.}
The object the credential prover produces is not a signature verification but a
\emph{zero-knowledge argument of knowledge} of a witness that makes a
signature-verification relation accept, with that witness hidden. Checking a signature is
easy once the signature is in hand; the difficulty this thesis instruments is to certify
that such a signature exists \emph{without revealing it, or the key it verifies under}.
Fix $\ppSig\leftarrow\Sig.\Setup(1^\lambda)$ and recall the efficiently decidable credential
relation of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. Every value to be signed is first mapped into the
message space by $\EncodeSig:\{0,1\}^{\ast}\to\MSig$, so $h_{U,TA}=H(A)\in\MSig$ and the
pseudonym is signed under the encoded message $m_{\mathsf{nym}}:=\EncodeSig(\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA})$;
the signatures are produced honestly by
$c_{U,TA}\leftarrow\Sig.\Sign(\ppSig,\sk_U,h_{U,TA})$ and
$\mathsf{psk}_{U,TA}\leftarrow\Sig.\Sign(\ppSig,\sk_U,m_{\mathsf{nym}})$. With this encoding the
credential relation reads
\[
  R_{\mathsf{cre}}^{\Sig}(x_{\mathsf{cre}};w_{\mathsf{cre}})=1
  \Longleftrightarrow
  \Pred_{\Verify}^{\Sig,\ppSig}(\pk_U,h_{U,TA},c_{U,TA})=1
  \ \land\
  \Pred_{\Verify}^{\Sig,\ppSig}(\pk_U,m_{\mathsf{nym}},\mathsf{psk}_{U,TA})=1,
\]
with public instance $x_{\mathsf{cre}}=(c_{U,TA},h_{U,TA},\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA})$ and \emph{private}
witness $w_{\mathsf{cre}}=(\pk_U,\mathsf{psk}_{U,TA})$. Membership is decidable in time polynomial in
$|x_{\mathsf{cre}}|+|w_{\mathsf{cre}}|$ by running $\Sig.\Verify$ twice. The credential prover supplies
a proof $\pi$ for ``$\exists\,w_{\mathsf{cre}}:R_{\mathsf{cre}}^{\Sig}(x_{\mathsf{cre}};w_{\mathsf{cre}})=1$''
that is zero-knowledge in $w_{\mathsf{cre}}$. Crucially $\pk_U$, though public \emph{by type}, is used here
as a hidden witness \emph{by role}: revealing it would tie the showing to the user's identity,
so anonymity requires $\pk_U\in w_{\mathsf{cre}}$, never in $x_{\mathsf{cre}}$. The same reading
carries to the showing relation $R_{\mathsf{show}}^{\Sig}$ with its $k+2$ predicate conjuncts,
where each pseudonym message is $m_{\mathsf{nym}}^{(j)}:=\EncodeSig(\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA}^{(j)})$ as in
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}.

\paragraph{Modelling the zkVM interface and journal-binding inside the game.}
The proof is produced by a zkVM whose universal relation is
$R_{\mathsf{vm}}\bigl((\mathsf{img},\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{pub}},\mathsf{out});(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{priv}})\bigr)=1$
iff $\mathsf{img}=\mathsf{digest}(\mathsf{P})$ and running guest $\mathsf{P}$ on
$(\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{pub}},\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{priv}})$ terminates emitting public output $\mathsf{out}$;
the public instance is $x_{\mathsf{vm}}=(\mathsf{img},\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{pub}},\mathsf{out})$ and the
witness $w_{\mathsf{vm}}=(\mathsf{P},\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{priv}})$. The host supplies inputs over a
\emph{read} channel and receives outputs over a \emph{commit} channel: values placed on the commit
channel enter $\mathsf{out}$ and become public, values read but not committed stay in
$\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{priv}}$ and remain hidden. In the BDEC instantiation $\sk_U$, $\pk_U$, and the
credential signatures enter only via the read channel and are never committed, so they remain in
$w_{\mathsf{vm}}$ across the entire relation, which is what keeps $w_{\mathsf{cre}}$ hidden. We make
the binding of the public statement an explicit predicate of the game:
\[
  \mathsf{JBind}(\mathsf{out},x)=1
  \iff
  \text{$x$ is committed on the commit channel, i.e.\ $x$ is recoverable from $\mathsf{out}$.}
  % \mathsf{JBind}: journal-binding indicator, written inline; no macro introduced.
\]
An accepting receipt certifies only ``$\exists\,w:R(x;w)=1$ for the $x$ that $\mathsf{JBind}$ pins
down''. The games below carry $\mathsf{JBind}$ as an explicit conjunct in the win condition, so that
they model the fixed/correct system in which $x_{\mathsf{cre}}$ is committed to the journal (a
cost-negligible change); the shipped prototype removes it, and we state that variant explicitly after
the games.

\paragraph{Oracles.}
All three experiments give the challenger an honest-user registry populated at setup (so that the
notion of an honestly generated user key pair is well defined, exactly as in
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}) and an issuance log $\mathcal L$ of tuples
$(\mathsf{ipk},\mathsf{upk},A,\mathsf{cred})$, together with the following two oracles, to which the
adversary $\Adv$ has \emph{adaptive} access. There is no corruption oracle: the source model of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} exposes only issuance and showing, and the cited BDEC bounds are proved
against exactly this interface.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Issue}}(\mathsf{upk},A)$: runs the honest issuance path. The teaching
authority signs
$\sigma_{\mathsf{cred}}\leftarrow\Sig.\Sign(\ppSig,\sk_{TA},\EncodeSig(\pk_U,\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA},A,\mathsf{aux}))$,
the ledger stores the record digest
$\rho_{U,TA}=H(\pk_{TA},\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA},A,\mathsf{aux},\sigma_{\mathsf{cred}})$, the challenger
appends $(\mathsf{ipk},\mathsf{upk},A,\mathsf{cred})$ to $\mathcal L$, and returns the issued
credential material.
\item $\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Show}}(i,\phi,A^\downarrow)$: for a registered honest user $i$, runs
honest $\BDEC.\mathsf{ShowCre}$ and returns the receipt $(x_{\mathsf{vm}},\mathsf{receipt})$, i.e.\ the
public statement
$x_{\mathsf{show}}=(A^\downarrow,(\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA}^{(j)})_{j=1}^{k},\mathsf{ppk}_{U,V})$ and the proof
$\pi$; the presentation predicate $\phi$ is adversary-chosen at show time.
\end{itemize}

\begin{gamebox}{Experiment $\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathsf{unf}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred},\Adv}(\lambda)$ (unforgeability)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The challenger samples $\parms=(\ppSig,\crs,\mathsf{img})\leftarrow\BDEC.\Setup(1^\lambda)$, where
$\mathsf{img}$ is the fixed guest-image digest of the credential guest, initialises
$\mathcal L\gets\emptyset$ and the honest-user registry, and gives $\parms$ and the public ledger roots
$(\mathsf{rt}_{\mathsf{cred},e},\mathsf{rt}_{TA,e},\mathsf{rt}_{\RL,e})$ to $\Adv$.
\item $\Adv$ interacts adaptively with $\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Issue}}$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Show}}$.
\item $\Adv$ outputs $(\mathsf{ipk}^\star,x^\star,\mathsf{receipt}^\star)$ with
$\mathsf{receipt}^\star=(\mathsf{out}^\star,\pi^\star)$ and public instance
$x_{\mathsf{vm}}^\star=(\mathsf{img},\mathsf{in}_{\mathsf{pub}}^\star,\mathsf{out}^\star)$.
\item The experiment outputs $1$ iff
\[
  \Pi_{\mathsf{vm}}.\Verify(\crs, x_{\mathsf{vm}}^\star, \pi^\star)=1
  \ \land\
  \mathsf{JBind}(\mathsf{out}^\star,x^\star)=1,
\]
the witness $w^\star$ extracted from $\pi^\star$ by the knowledge extractor satisfies
$R_{\mathsf{cre}}^{\Sig}(x^\star;w^\star)=1$ (equivalently $R_{\mathsf{show}}^{\Sig}$ for a showing),
$\mathsf{ipk}^\star$ is an authorised teaching authority (a member under the published root
$\mathsf{rt}_{TA,e}$), and the forgery is \emph{fresh}: there is no tuple
$(\mathsf{ipk}^\star,\mathsf{upk}^\star,A^\star,\mathsf{cred}^\star)\in\mathcal L$ and no honestly
generated user key pair $(\mathsf{usk}^\star,\mathsf{upk}^\star)$ accounting for $w^\star$.
\end{enumerate}
$\displaystyle
\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{unf}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}(\Adv)
=\Pr\!\bigl[\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathsf{unf}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred},\Adv}(\lambda)=1\bigr].$
The extraction step in~4 is where knowledge soundness ($\epsilon_{\mathrm{KS}}$) is spent, and the
$\mathsf{JBind}$ conjunct is what pins the extracted $x^\star$ to a committed statement.
\end{gamebox}

\begin{gamebox}{Experiment $\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathsf{anon}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred},\Adv}(\lambda)$ (anonymity)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Setup as above; $\Adv$ receives $\parms$, the roots, and adaptive access to
$\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Issue}}$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Show}}$.
\item $\Adv$ outputs two honestly issued credentials
$(\mathsf{cred}_0,\mathsf{usk}_0,A_0)$, $(\mathsf{cred}_1,\mathsf{usk}_1,A_1)$, a predicate $\phi$ with
$\phi(A_0)=\phi(A_1)=1$, and disclosed attributes $A^\downarrow$.
\item Well-formedness: the challenge is admissible only if
$\mathsf{Leak}(\mathsf{stmt}_0)=\mathsf{Leak}(\mathsf{stmt}_1)$, where $\mathsf{Leak}(\cdot)$ projects the
statement onto its intentionally-disclosed components, namely $A^\downarrow$, the teaching-authority
pseudonym multiset $\{\mathsf{ppk}_{U,TA}^{(j)}\}$, and any linking tag carried in $\phi$; the
verifier-facing pseudonym $\mathsf{ppk}_{U,V}$ is freshly sampled at showing time.
\item The challenger samples $b\leftarrow\{0,1\}$ and returns
$(\mathsf{stmt},\mathsf{receipt}_b)\leftarrow\BDEC.\mathsf{ShowCre}$ for $\mathsf{cred}_b$.
\item $\Adv$, with continued oracle access, outputs $b'$.
\end{enumerate}
$\displaystyle
\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{anon}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}(\Adv)
=\Bigl|\Pr[b'=b]-\tfrac12\Bigr|.$
This advantage is made small only when the receipt is zero-knowledge in $w_{\mathsf{vm}}$; on the
shipped succinct-STARK path it is not (Section~\ref{ssec:zk-gap}), so the bound is sound but not
effective there. Non-cryptographic side channels (timing, ledger-access and revocation-update
patterns, wallet metadata) are outside this model.
\end{gamebox}

\begin{gamebox}{Experiment $\mathsf{Exp}^{\mathsf{unlink}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred},\Adv}(\lambda)$ (unlinkability)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Setup and the \emph{same} oracle interface as the unforgeability experiment
($\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Issue}}$ and $\mathcal O_{\mathsf{Show}}$), available adaptively to $\Adv$.
\item The challenger creates two pseudonym key pairs $(\mathsf{psk}^0,\mathsf{ppk}^0)$,
$(\mathsf{psk}^1,\mathsf{ppk}^1)$ and an attribute set $A^\downarrow$, samples $b\leftarrow\{0,1\}$, and
issues a \emph{single} credential $c^{(b)}$ with respect to $\mathsf{ppk}^{(b)}$ on $A^\downarrow$,
forwarding it to $\Adv$.
\item $\Adv$ outputs $b'$.
\end{enumerate}
$\displaystyle
\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{unlink}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}(\Adv)
=\Bigl|\Pr[b'=b]-\tfrac12\Bigr|.$
When the statements intentionally carry a public linking tag, linkability is allowed only as that tag
determines. The single-transcript structure is why the one-transcript simulation of
Section~\ref{sec:security} applies once, giving unlinkability the same bound as anonymity.
\end{gamebox}

\paragraph{The shipped-prototype game ($\mathsf{JBind}$ removed).}
The measured artifact commits only the verification outcome, a single boolean, so
$\mathsf{JBind}(\mathsf{out}^\star,x^\star)=0$ for every nontrivial $x^\star$; modelling it means
deleting the $\mathsf{JBind}$ conjunct from the unforgeability win condition above. With that conjunct
gone the extracted $x^\star$ is unpinned, and the adversary trivially satisfies statement-binding with
any $x^\star$ consistent with the one committed boolean, so an accepting receipt attests merely
``two signatures verify under \emph{some} witness''. The prototype is therefore a functional benchmark,
not a statement-bound verifier; committing $x_{\mathsf{cre}}$ to the journal restores the conjunct and
closes the gap.

The assembled bounds of this chapter are sums of leaf error terms. It is worth stating, term by term,
what \emph{form} of bound each source actually delivers, because the honest answer is that none of the
five is a reproduced concrete or parameterised expression: every one resolves to ``negligible'', and
three of them (\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{AIR}}\), \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{sZK}}\), and \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}}\)
on the shipped stack) are \emph{open or not-met} rather than established. Table~\ref{tab:leaf-errors}
records this. In particular the only probabilistic figures attached to any leaf-error term in this sum,
\(2^{-199}\) per vector and \(2^{-191}\) aggregate, are the positive-direction \emph{test coverage} of
the executor-equivalence suite under a uniform-wrong-output model; they bound random disagreement on the
sampled set, not the rejection of a malformed trace, and are therefore \emph{not} a soundness bound on
\(\epsilon_{\mathrm{AIR}}\).

With these forms, the assembled bounds read, in the classical random-oracle model,
\begin{align}
  \mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{unf}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}(\Adv)
  &\le \epsilon_{\mathrm{EUF}} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{KS}} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{AIR}},
  \label{eq:adv-unf}\\
  \mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{anon}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}(\Adv)
  &\le \epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{sZK}},
  \label{eq:adv-anon}
\end{align}
and the same bound $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}}+\epsilon_{\mathrm{sZK}}$ holds for
$\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathsf{unlink}}_{\mathsf{zkVM\text{-}Cred}}(\Adv)$, whose game forwards a single challenge
credential so the one-transcript simulation applies once. Unforgeability is negligible whenever its
three terms are, of which $\epsilon_{\mathrm{AIR}}$ is the open prong. Anonymity and unlinkability are
negligible only when $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}}$ is, which the shipped stack does not deliver
(Section~\ref{ssec:zk-gap}); on that path Equation~\eqref{eq:adv-anon} is sound but not made
effective.

What this chapter delivers is two results, ordered as in the abstract: a \emph{cost criterion} for when
a precompile pays, which is the chapter's reusable, positive contribution, and a \emph{dual obstruction}
to deployable post-quantum anonymity on the studied stack, which is the deployability finding. The cost
criterion (Proposition~\ref{prop:precompile-pays}, Equation~\eqref{eq:precompile-pays}) is a single
inequality evaluated at two operating points: against standalone PLUM verification, which does not
complete (out of memory at ${\approx}1$m$45$s), the bespoke Griffin-$\mathbb{F}_p^{192}$ precompile
removes millions of guest cycles per event at a per-event chip area on the order of $10^{4}$ cells and
recovers a finite $14.25$-minute succinct proof at $\lambda=80$; against a software SHA-3 control whose
hash costs the substrate no chip area at all, the same inequality places the precompiled arm
\emph{behind} the control ($14.25$ versus $13.05$~min, ${\approx}1.09\times$ wall, $1.13\times$ cycles,
$n{=}3$). The precompile is the instrument, not the result: the criterion is the reusable contribution,
and it is the demonstrated \emph{instance} --- the with/without-precompile inversion that the accounting
postdicts --- that is the one falsified prediction, not the criterion itself. The dual obstruction
(Proposition~\ref{prop:wall}) is likewise real and has two independent horns: the feasible bare-STARK
receipt is not zero-knowledge, and the only wraps the toolchains expose are pairing-based, hence not
post-quantum. Horn~1 is a recursion-shape provisioning wall, a one-time build cost (${\approx}51$~h of
regeneration) and \emph{not} a memory bound; Horn~2 is a primitive limit no memory or time budget
removes. Within the literature we surveyed we did not find this conjunction stated for post-quantum
anonymous credentials, and surfacing it is the second contribution.

We do \emph{not} claim a delivered anonymity or unlinkability guarantee on the evaluated stack. Those
guarantees are conditional and sound, but their bound is non-informative (vacuous) on this stack because
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}}$ is not made small: Equation~\eqref{eq:adv-anon} bounds anonymity by
$\epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}}$, and on the target hardware that error is not driven small, because the
succinct receipt has no established zero-knowledge simulator and the wrap that would supply one is not
produced as the toolchain ships (Horn~1). Aligning with Proposition~\ref{prop:wall}, the construction's
anonymity is therefore ``sound but not made effective on this stack: no proof the stack produces drives
its $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ZK}}$ term negligible.'' Two further honesty caveats bound the claim. First, the
shipped prototype commits the verification outcome but not the public statement $x_{\mathsf{cre}}$ to the
journal, so an accepting receipt attests ``two signatures verify under some witness'' without binding to
a specific statement; until $x_{\mathsf{cre}}$ is committed the artifact is a functional benchmark, not a
statement-bound credential verifier. This statement-binding requirement is disclosed in
Section~\ref{sec:system} and carried as an explicit conditional in Lemma~\ref{lem:relation-preserve}, and
made an explicit win-condition conjunct ($\mathsf{JBind}$) in the experiments above. Second, ``the user's
long-term key and signatures enter only via the read channel and are never committed'' is a statement
about the witness channel, not an unlinkability result; it is the mechanism that \emph{could} deliver
anonymity under a zero-knowledge wrap, and nothing more absent that wrap. The contribution is the
criterion and the obstruction, both established; the anonymity guarantee is stated as what it is, a
conditional that the measured substrate does not discharge.
